# Double Cropping: Literature Notes

## Definition
Double cropping is the practice of growing two crops sequentially on the same field within one growing season.

## Problematic Combinations

By "problematic" we mean double crop combinations containing crops from different vulnerability categories, requiring a classification rule.

| Code | Combination | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| 232 | Lettuce/Cotton | Vulnerable + GM |
| 230 | Lettuce/Durum Wheat | Vulnerable + Tolerant |
| 233 | Lettuce/Barley | Vulnerable + Tolerant |

By default, we apply the priority rule GM > Tolerant > Vulnerable. However, since lettuce is the only vulnerable crop across all three combinations, the key question is simply: **is lettuce present during the regional Dicamba exposure period?**

## Crop Calendars

### Lettuce
Planting: late August–December. Harvest: December–March. Fields cleared by April.

Source: University of Arizona ACIS, Lettuce Crop Management
https://acis.cals.arizona.edu/agricultural-ipm/vegetables/lettuce/crop-management

### Soybean and Cotton
Both crops occupy fields during late spring and summer.

- Soybean: planted April–June, active growth May–August, harvested September–October.
  Source: https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/soybean-production-calendar.html

- Cotton: planted April–May, active growth May–August, harvested September–December.
  Source: https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/crop-production/cotton-production-calendar/

## Dicamba Application Window

**Pre-2016:** Dicamba product were only registered in non-Over-the-Top (non-OTT) applications.  Thus, they were limited to pre-plant, burndown, and early-season weed control, and hence applied in early spring before crop emergence.

**Post-2016:** In 2016, Monsanto genetically engineered soybeans and cotton to resist to Dicamba, allowing OTT applications directly onto actively growing crops during late spring and summer (May–July).

Source:
- https://www.epa.gov/pesticides/epa-provides-update-over-top-uses-dicamba
- https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/epa-reapproves-dangerous-drift-prone-pesticide-dicamba-2026-02-06/

The EPA Registration Decision of October 31, 2018 (covering the 2019-2020 seasons) introduced a formal prohibition on OTT spraying 60 days or later after planting cotton and 45 days or later after planting soybeans. Importantly, the EPA noted this was not a significant burden for growers as *"the majority of OTT spraying already occurs within these timeframes."* This confirms that even before the formal cutoff, Dicamba exposure was already concentrated between planting and approximately 60 days after, which corresponds to May–July.

Source: EPA Registration Decision for the Continuation of Uses of Dicamba on Dicamba Tolerant Cotton and Soybean. (PDF available on references section of this repository).

## Conclusion

Lettuce is harvested by March and replanted in August, which is outside (or with only a small overlap) the Dicamba exposure window in both pre- and post-2016 regimes.
Combined with the negligible area of problematic double crop combinations (0.0025% of US agricultural land), it is with confidence that we can classify them in the non-vulnerable categories.
